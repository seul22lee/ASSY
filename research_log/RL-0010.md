# RL-0010 — Topological anchors: what a feature is attached to

**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Contract touched:** `TopologyKind`, `TopologicalAnchor`, `SpatialConstraint.anchor`, `PlacedPiece.anchor`

## Summary

Stage 04 reasoned about regions and membership but not about topology. A relation
recorded *which* elements related and *what kind* of relation it was, never *what
it was attached to*. Every relation and every local element now carries a
`TopologicalAnchor` naming a face, edge, axis, corridor, contact surface, boundary
or volume — with partial resolution recorded honestly where a parameter remains
free.

## Trigger

A review question: does Stage 04 encode enough that Stage 05 never has to
rediscover where a hinge, guide or contact belongs?

## Evidence

**13 of 15 spatial constraints carried `axis=None, station=None`.** The fields
existed and were unpopulated for all but two. A joint recorded
`attached_to=['input_member']` — the host, never the place on it: no face, no
edge, no axis.

The decisive evidence was in the renderer. To draw anything it computed
`_surface_point` and `_contact_centre`, deriving contact locations from block
geometry — **and discarded them**. Stage 05 would have had to redo that derivation
from the same inputs, and any difference between the two derivations would be a
silent disagreement about where the mechanism is.

## Root cause

The spatial vocabulary was volumetric only: zones, regions, membership. "In the
retention region" locates a catch to a volume; a catch is not in a volume, it is
*on a surface*. Nothing in the schema could express attachment, so attachment was
left to whoever drew or built the thing next.

A second consequence: the renderer's own extents were fractions of the host
(0.42 for a joint, 0.30 for a feature) — flagged in RL-0009 as conventions with no
engineering content. They existed because there was nothing better to derive from.

## Design decision

`TopologyKind {FACE, EDGE, AXIS, CORRIDOR, CONTACT_SURFACE, VOLUME, BOUNDARY}`
and a `TopologicalAnchor` carrying hosts, axis, faces, station and span.

**Partial resolution is a first-class outcome.** A revolute joint on a moving
boundary narrows to "an edge of the +Z face" with `resolved=False` and
`open_parameter="which edge of the named face"`. Narrowing from a whole member to
one named face is real information; choosing among that face's four edges would be
deciding geometry on no evidence. Recording the freedom is the honest middle.

One general distinction proved necessary: **a revolute pair carried on a surface
is an edge; a revolute pair through a solid is an axis.** A line on a surface is a
line *of* that surface. This is derived from the declared `moving_boundary` role,
not from any element name.

Renderer geometry is now driven by anchor kind rather than host fractions, which
also discharges the RL-0009 limitation: an edge draws as a line, an axis as a line
at a station, a corridor as a band spanning the travel, a contact surface as a thin
patch between two members.

## Stage ownership

| Field | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `RELATION_ANCHOR`, `JOINT_ANCHOR` | knowledge base | that a revolute pair lives on a line is mechanical, true in any product |
| `TopologicalAnchor` | schema | shared vocabulary between the stage that resolves it and the stages that consume it |
| anchor *resolution* | Stage 04 | which axis, which face, which station is a placement question |
| anchor-driven geometry | renderer | drawing follows the topology it is given |

## Generalization

The anchor kind is looked up by relation kind and by permitted motion — never by
element name. A test asserts **every** `SpatialRelationKind` has an anchor rule, so
a new relation cannot be added without deciding where it lives.

Eight synthetic tests use a rotary airlock valve and a sliding plate, neither in
the catalogue: a revolute joint on a solid anchors to an axis, the same joint on a
surface anchors to an edge, a prismatic joint anchors to a corridor spanning the
travel, a feature anchors to a contact surface, a partial anchor names its open
parameter, and an anchor never invents a resolution it lacks.

## Freeze status

Not frozen. Stage 05 does not yet read anchors, so the rediscovery this revision
prevents is prevented only in principle until it does.

## Remaining issues

- `TopologicalAnchor` allows any combination of its optional fields; nothing
  enforces that an AXIS anchor carries an axis or that an EDGE carries faces.
- BM-002 leaves one contact surface unresolved because neither host carries a
  declared face — correct behaviour, but it means contact resolution depends on
  face assignment having reached both members.
- A joint with three or more hosts still uses only the first two.
- `FACE` as an anchor kind is declared but currently unreachable: no relation rule
  yields it, since boundary crossings resolve to `BOUNDARY`.

## Next validation target

Stage 05 consuming `element_class` and `anchor` together. A joint is not a part to
be dimensioned, and an anchor is where the part it *does* dimension must attach.
