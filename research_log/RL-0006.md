# RL-0006 — Spatial contract repair

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Contract touched:** `ProductReferenceFrame`, `BoundaryFace`, `AxisStation`, `AccessPath`, `MotionKind`, `SpatialConstraint`
**Supersedes:** the `ReferenceFrame` and `MotionClass` introduced in [RL-0004](RL-0004.md)

## Summary

Four benchmark-independent revisions, proposed and reviewed before implementation.
A product-relative reference frame with signed faces and geometric axis stations;
general access paths; explicit per-element motion kind carried through the whole
handoff; and typed interface-realization constraints that Stage 04 checks against
its own placement.

## Trigger

The four defects reported in [RL-0005](RL-0005.md), plus an explicit instruction
to repair the architecture contracts rather than patch the rendered examples. A
proposal was written and reviewed first; nothing was implemented until the rules
were agreed.

## Evidence

From RL-0005: BM-001's hinge and lid could sit on opposite sides while the
blueprint recorded a joint between them; BM-002's payload region rendered outside
a sealed housing with no route in; two supports collapsed onto one end station;
`BOUNDARY` could not say which face.

During implementation, two further pieces of evidence emerged. The first
constraint pass produced **three false violations** on BM-002 — a crank
"unreachable" from the shaft it drives. The second was that `housing` was
consuming an axis station, pushing real reactors onto the wrong end.

## Root cause

The blueprint's vocabulary was too coarse to state what the architecture already
knew. Zones expressed distance from an axis but not adjacency, which end, or which
face. Motion kind was inferred from role tags rather than declared. And nothing
converted a declared interface into a *spatial* demand, so a joint and a placement
could contradict each other silently.

## Design decision

**Frame.** Three signed product axes. The frame names the product's own
directions and never claims which way it faces in the world — a test asserts the
derivation text contains no world-orientation word.

**Faces.** Identified by signed face (`+X … -Z`) and carrying semantic roles.
Roles never force exclusivity: when one element carries two roles they merge onto
one face with `shared=True`, because a container opened and loaded through one
aperture is normal engineering. What matters is that sharing is recorded as a
decision rather than an accident.

**Stations.** Geometric semantics only — `negative_end`, `mid_span`,
`positive_end`, `range_min`, `range_max`. Functional-chain start/end was
explicitly rejected as a substitute: which end the input enters is independent of
which end a stop acts at.

**Constraints.** Ten relation kinds, deliberately **no generic `COINCIDENT`
rule** — a threaded pair and a fixed attachment place different demands, and
collapsing them would make the check unfalsifiable. Each row is stated as the
statics or kinematics it encodes.

Two rules were added in response to the implementation evidence, both general:

- **Boundary spanning.** An element in a boundary-crossing interface is present on
  both sides — a shaft entering an enclosure is genuinely outside and inside at
  once. `FLANKING` remains never on-axis, so this removes the false positives
  without weakening the real check.
- **Shells take no station.** A shell bounds the whole envelope rather than
  sitting at an end.

`NOT_CHECKABLE` was made a distinct status and is **never counted as a pass**.

## Stage ownership

| Field | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `RoleTemplate.motion` | knowledge base | how an element moves is mechanism knowledge |
| `FunctionalPart.motion_kind`, `spatial_constraints` | Stage 02 | "a threaded pair is coaxial" holds in any product |
| `ProductPiece.motion_kind`, `access_paths` | Stage 03 | it owns enclosure, load-path ownership and assembly order — the three access triggers |
| frame, faces, stations, constraint *status* | Stage 04 | first stage holding a placement to check against |

## Generalization

Access paths derive from three structural triggers only, none naming a product:
an access obligation on a non-external element; a load path originating outside
the declared element set; a piece enclosed at assembly. Motion kind is declared
per catalogue role and kept orthogonal to engineering role tags — a lid is a
`moving_boundary` whose motion kind is `rotation`, and a test asserts the two
never collapse.

**23 synthetic tests** exercise every abstraction on products that do not exist in
this repository and mechanisms not in the catalogue: a peristaltic dosing head, a
sealed instrument with a consumable cartridge, a two-bearing spindle, and abstract
element pairs.

## Validation performed

All six required detections confirmed on regenerated artifacts:

| Detection | BM-001 | BM-002 |
|---|---|---|
| connected opening interface / moving closure | `shared_axis` **VIOLATED** (correct) | — |
| external-to-internal access route | satisfied | `ACCESS_PATH_UNMET` (correct) |
| distinct axis ends | `mid_span`, `range_min` | `negative_end`, `positive_end`, `range_min`, `mid_span` |
| boundary-face assignment | `+Z` loading+operating (shared), `-Z` seating | `-Y` operating, `+Y` service, `+Z` loading, `-Z` seating |
| rotational vs translational envelopes | cylindrical | cylindrical + prismatic |
| unrealizable relationships | 1 of 10 violated | 0 of 15 violated |

**183 deterministic tests pass**; 5 live-model regressions separate. Source-text
invariance, absence of benchmark lookup, absence of prose parsing and
byte-identical rendering all re-verified.

## Freeze status

**Ready for visual-spatial review freeze, with one caveat.** The representation is
stable and proven independently of both benchmarks. The caveat: Stage 04 *detects*
violations but does not *use* them when placing, so a blueprint can be internally
contradictory and still be emitted — honestly labelled, but contradictory.

## Remaining issues

- BM-001's `shared_axis` violation is real and unfixed. Detecting it was the goal;
  resolving it requires constraint-driven placement.
- BM-002's payload path remains unmet — a Stage 03 architecture gap, reported.
- A bidirectional travel can be bounded at one extreme only; representable now,
  but no rule yet requires both.
- BM-002's single guide is still unflagged: the DOF / load-reaction model
  (proposal 5 of the review) was explicitly out of scope for this cycle.

## Next validation target

Either constraint-driven placement — Stage 04 satisfying constraints rather than
only checking them — or the deferred DOF and reacted-load model, which is the
general form of "a moment cannot be reacted at a single station".
