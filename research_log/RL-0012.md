# RL-0012 — Observability of motion is a property of the model, not the drawing

*Date:* 2026-07-28
*Trigger:* BM-101 rendered three states of an indexing mechanism that were pixel-identical.

## Why

An indexing mechanism exists to move its output to a discrete station and hold it
there. BM-101 produced a correct joint set, a correct state sequence, and three
drawings in which nothing moved. Every prior benchmark had hidden this, because
BM-001 and BM-002 both move something's *position* — a lid swings, a platform
travels. An output that only changes *orientation* was new, and it exposed three
separate assumptions that had never been tested.

None of them is a Geneva problem. Each is a general defect that BM-101 happened to
be the first case to reach.

### 1. A cycle was assumed to be a full revolution

`_q` advanced a body turning in place by `2π` between bounded states. For a
continuous drive that is right. For an intermittent one it is exactly wrong: the
output advances **one station**, and `2π` is indistinguishable from `0`, so the
model asserted that `dwell` and `indexed` are the same configuration. They are the
two configurations the mechanism is *for*.

The advance is now taken from the family's declared continuity, and driver is
distinguished from driven by position in the element chain — so a crank still
turns once per index while the thing it indexes moves one step. Station count is
not derivable from anything stated, so it is recorded as a free parameter rather
than presented as a decision.

### 2. Rotation and translation project oppositely, and the renderer knew neither

The renderer drew fixed front and side elevations. Both contain the vertical axis,
and **a rotation about an axis is invisible in any plane containing it**. The view
was not showing the motion; it could not have.

The first correction ranked axes by coordinate magnitude, which picked BM-101's
fast input over its slow output, and treated translation like rotation — choosing
for BM-002 the one view that hides its travel. The correct statement is asymmetric:
a rotation is visible only *perpendicular* to its axis, a translation only in a
plane *containing* it. Views are now derived from the joint driving the output
element, because a mechanism exists to produce its output motion. BM-101 gets the
plan view, BM-002 keeps its elevation, BM-001 its section — one rule, three
different and correct answers.

### 3. A contact was declared between two axes, where contact cannot occur

`intermittent_indexing_pair` declared a `CONTACT_PAIR` between two bodies that each
turn on their own axis. At an axis, surface speed and moment arm are both zero:
nothing can be transmitted there. The engagement happens at a **radius**, through
an element the driver carries — a pin, a tooth, a lobe, a cam rise. `pawl_advance_pair`
already had one and was fine; the indexing pair had a contact with nowhere to occur.

Added as `_engagement_element`, and features hosted on a turning body are now seated
at a radius so the host's rotation carries them.

## What was tried and rejected

**Giving the driven side an engagement element too.** This is correct — the
reference concept has a pin on the driver *and* slots on the wheel, and without an
off-axis element an axisymmetric body turning about its own axis of symmetry has no
observable state at all. Adding it flipped BM-101's selection to `pawl_advance_pair`,
because part count feeds the score and enriching a family therefore penalises it.

Reverted rather than fixed by reweighting. This is the same failure mode as RL-0004:
the score is punishing description quality, and tuning the weight would hide that
while leaving the next enrichment to trip over it. Recorded as open.

## Consequence

The indexed member still shows no visible rotation, and that is now an honest
statement rather than a bug: it is a `SHAFT`, and a shaft turning about its own
axis genuinely has no observable pose change. The model is correct
(`row0: [1,0,0] → [0.5,-0.866,0]`, exactly 60°); what is missing is a driven-side
angular datum, which is blocked on the selection-scoring issue above.

## Also corrected

`_drive_completion` bundled travel completion with an axis redirect, putting
interfaces that name a `redirect_member` into `flexible_tension_drive`, which
declares no such role. A drive needs a redirect only when its input and conversion
axes differ; a routed flexible member changes direction without one. Split into
`_axis_redirect`. This was a live contract violation — an interface naming
undeclared elements — caught by the Stage 02 machine-consumability test.

## Supersedes

Nothing. Extends RL-0011 (estimate provenance) with state observability.
Leaves RL-0004's scoring concern open and now twice-evidenced.
