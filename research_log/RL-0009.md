# RL-0009 — Spatial-first, semantics-on-top

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Contract touched:** none. Renderer only (`assy/conceptrender.py`, `assy/conceptcoverage.py`)
**Corrects:** the over-correction introduced in [RL-0008](RL-0008.md)

## Summary

RL-0008 correctly distinguished bodies from joints and features, then drew the
wrong conclusion from it: joints and features were removed from the spatial layout
entirely and replaced by symbols. They now keep a drawn spatial extent, derived
from the bodies they touch, with the glyph layered on top of it.

## Trigger

Review of the RL-0008 output. Motion visualisation had improved and local
functional features had lost their spatial representation.

## Evidence

After RL-0008, `_layout()` filtered to `element_class == "body"`. For an
architecture of five elements with two bodies, one joint and two features, three
of the five vanished from every spatial view and survived only as markers. A
reviewer could see *that* a hinge existed but not *where* it was or how much room
it took.

## Root cause

A conflation in RL-0008's own reasoning. "A joint has no **independent** position"
was treated as "a joint has no **extent**". Those are different claims. A hinge
occupies the edge where two members meet — a definite location with definite, if
small, size. A threaded pair occupies its engagement length. What a joint lacks is
a position *of its own*, derived from a zone; what it has is a position *derived
from what it connects*.

Removing the extent also removed the reviewer's ability to ask the question the
picture exists to answer: does this fit.

## Design decision

**Spatial-first, semantics-on-top.** Every functional element and every localized
interface occupies a region with extent. A glyph annotates that region; it never
substitutes for one. What differs by class is only how extent is derived:

| Class | Extent | Derived from |
|---|---|---|
| body | own | its zone slot |
| joint | derived | spans between the bodies it connects |
| feature | derived | a patch on its host's surface |
| interface | derived | the engagement region between the pair |

Derived extents render **dashed**, marking them as inferred from hosts rather than
stated by the architecture. This keeps the drawing honest about which of its
claims the contracts actually make.

A second correction followed from drawing them. Placing a contact at the midpoint
of two centroids puts the contact between a closure and a shell *inside* the
volume the shell encloses. Two members touch at their **surfaces**, so an
engagement region is now centred between the nearest surfaces of the pair.

RL-0008's finding that a joint does not sweep a volume is unaffected and retained:
having an extent and sweeping a volume are separate properties.

## Stage ownership

Nothing moved; no contract changed. Extent derivation is a drawing convention and
belongs to the renderer, which is also why it can be corrected without a
migration. The kinematic class it reads is Stage 02's, per RL-0008.

## Generalization

The derivation is stated over `element_class` and `attached_to`, never over an
element name. `LOCALIZED_RELATIONS` names the relation kinds that denote a contact
at a place — as opposed to a routed link or a reachability claim, which relate two
elements without occupying a region between them.

Five tests enforce the principle directly: every joint and feature has a drawn
extent, no extent is degenerate, a joint is strictly smaller than the bodies it
connects, every localized relation produces an engagement region, and a contact
computed between a lid and an enclosing shell lands on the shell boundary rather
than sinking into it.

## Validation performed

- Both benchmarks regenerated; joints, features and engagements visible with
  extent in every spatial view, glyphs layered on top.
- Coverage remains complete: four new glyph names registered, no unmapped fields,
  no orphan glyphs.
- Rendering remains byte-identical across runs.
- **212 deterministic tests pass.**

## Freeze status

Renderer not frozen; the underlying contracts retain their RL-0008 status.

## Remaining issues

- Derived extents use fixed fractions of the host (0.42 for a joint, 0.30 for a
  feature, 0.34 for an engagement). These are drawing conventions with no
  engineering content, but they are also not *stated* anywhere as conventions the
  way the nominal envelope is.
- A joint with three or more hosts uses only the first two to compute its span.
- Labels on co-located derived regions still overprint despite staggering.

## Next validation target

Unchanged from RL-0008: whether Stage 05 should consume `element_class`. A joint
is not a part to be dimensioned, and treating it as one is the same category error
one stage later.
