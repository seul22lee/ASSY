# RL-0008 — Kinematic element class: bodies, joints and features

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Contract touched:** `ElementClass`, `FunctionalPart.permits_motion`, `PlacedPiece.attached_to`
**Supersedes:** the `UNSUPPORTED_SPAN` check introduced in [RL-0004](RL-0004.md)

## Summary

The representation gained a kinematic element class — **body**, **joint**,
**feature** — orthogonal to functional role and manufacturing kind. A joint now
declares the relative motion it permits, joints and features are located by the
bodies they touch rather than placed independently, and a body's declared motion
must be grounded in a joint that permits it.

## Trigger

A review of whether the renderer exposed the mechanical information already
present. It did not, but the deeper finding was that some of the information was
wrong in a way no renderer could fix.

## Evidence

`opening_interface` — a hinge — carried `moving=True, motion_kind=rotation` and
was allocated its own swept volume. **A hinge does not sweep a volume; the door it
carries does.** The same class of error applied to `guide_member`,
`input_support` and `thrust_support` (all joints, all given bulk and independent
positions) and to `detent_feature`, `travel_stop` and `opening_stop` (all local
features, all consuming whole regions).

Two consequences were measurable. BM-001 emitted a swept volume for a hinge, and
the RL-0005 finding "the hinge sits opposite the lid it carries" was only
expressible because a joint could be *placed* somewhere at all.

## Root cause

The representation had a **functional** role (`MechanismRole`) and a
**manufacturing** kind (`PieceKind`) but no **kinematic** classification. Without
it, a relationship between two bodies was indistinguishable from a body, so it
acquired the three properties only a body has: bulk, an independent position, and
a swept volume.

Four further gaps followed from the same omission:

- a motion axis had nowhere to live, so the renderer guessed it from a face normal
- a joint's permitted motion was unstated, so no check could ask whether a
  declared motion was possible
- a feature had no host, so it was drawn free-standing
- an interface glyph had no location, so it was drawn between centroids

## Design decision

`ElementClass {BODY, JOINT, FEATURE}` declared per catalogue role — the same
ownership argument as `motion_kind` in RL-0006: whether an element is a joint is a
property of the mechanism family, and a renderer deciding it would be inventing
kinematics.

`permits_motion` on the joint anchors the motion axis where it belongs. A joint's
own `motion_kind` became `FIXED`: a bearing race does not rotate, it lets
something else rotate. This removed the spurious swept volumes at source.

`attached_to` is derived by Stage 04 from declared interfaces and obligations, not
stored upstream — it is a resolution against the placement, not new knowledge.

**`UNSUPPORTED_SPAN` was removed, not kept alongside.** It assumed radial support
was the only way to support rotation, and it false-positived on any body carried
by a hinge. `UNGROUNDED_MOTION` — a declared motion with no joint permitting it —
is the general form and subsumes it.

## Stage ownership

| Field | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `RoleTemplate.element_class`, `.permits` | knowledge base | kinematic class is a property of the mechanism family |
| `FunctionalPart.element_class`, `.permits_motion` | Stage 02 | "a hinge is a joint" holds in any product |
| `ProductPiece.*` | Stage 03 | forwarding, no new knowledge |
| `PlacedPiece.attached_to` | Stage 04 | derived against the placement |
| joint/feature glyphs | renderer | drawing follows the class it is given |

## Generalization

Nothing keys on an element name. The class is declared per role template; the
grounding rule is stated over `permits_motion` versus `motion_kind`; localization
reads declared interfaces. A latent contradiction the split exposed was corrected
generically: `tension_member` and `tension_link` were flagged `moving=True` while
declaring `motion=fixed`, and now declare translation and rotation_translation.

**Eight synthetic tests** on a cam-driven shutter blade and a rotating slider —
neither in the catalogue — assert that a joint does not sweep, that a joint and a
feature are located by what they touch, that a translation-only joint does not
ground a rotation, that an unhosted feature is reported, that a fixed body needs
no joint, and that compliant deformation is grounded by the element's own
compliance.

## Validation performed

| | BM-001 before | after | BM-002 before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| swept volumes | 2 (incl. a hinge) | **1** | 4 | 4 |
| constraint violations | 1 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| issues | 5 | **0** | 16 | 15 |

**207 deterministic tests pass.** Source-text invariance, absence of benchmark
lookup and deterministic rendering all re-verified.

BM-001's `shared_axis` violation disappeared. This is **not** a patch: the
violation was expressible only because a joint could be placed away from what it
joins, and that placement was the category error. The substantive check it was
standing in for — is this motion actually possible — is now `UNGROUNDED_MOTION`,
which is stronger because it applies to every motion kind rather than to radial
support alone. BM-002 correctly reports `transmission_shaft` as ungrounded: it
rotates with no joint permitting rotation, a real catalogue gap.

## Freeze status

Not frozen. The class split is sound but its consequences downstream (Stage 05
consumes `parts`, which now carry a class it does not read) are untested.

## Remaining issues

- BM-002's `transmission_shaft` still has no bearing — a genuine catalogue gap now
  reported by a general rule instead of a special one.
- BM-001 renders sparsely: with 2 bodies, 1 joint and 2 features, the honest
  picture is nearly empty. Correct, but it makes the sheet look under-populated.
- Stage 05 does not read `element_class`, so it still treats joints as parts.
- `permits_motion` carries a single motion kind; a joint permitting two
  simultaneous freedoms cannot be expressed.

## Next validation target

Whether Stage 05 should consume `element_class` — a joint is not a part to be
dimensioned, and treating it as one is the same category error one stage later.
