# RL-0011 — Derived placement: why a feature is where it is

**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Contract touched:** `LocationBasis`, `LocationDerivation`, `SpatialRelationKind.SEPARATED_ALONG_AXIS`
**Supersedes:** the counter-based station assignment introduced in [RL-0006](RL-0006.md)

## Summary

RL-0010 derived the *kind* of topological entity a feature attaches to. It did not
derive the *location parameters*, which were still inherited from region assignment
and an alternating counter. Every localized feature now cites the mechanical
relationship that put it where it is, and where the mechanism constrains without
determining, the freedom is recorded rather than resolved.

## Trigger

The observation that Stage 04 still placed features as spatial objects and then
attached semantics, rather than deriving location from the relationship.

## Evidence

The decisive line was in `_stations`:

    AxisStation.NEGATIVE_END if n_support % 2 == 0 else AxisStation.POSITIVE_END

**Whether a bearing sat at the negative or positive end was decided by declaration
order.** A `% 2` counter was producing an engineering statement.

Following it revealed the same pattern upstream: a joint's zone came from
`ZONE_BY_REGION_KIND` via a `MechanismRole` table in Stage 03, and the anchor then
read that zone back out. The pipeline was Region → Visualization → Mechanical
label, exactly as diagnosed.

The counter also **fabricated a relationship**. In BM-002 it placed `input_support`
at one end and `thrust_support` at the other as though they were a couple. They
serve different elements and were never a pair; the alternation invented the
association.

## Root cause

The representation had no way to say *why* something is where it is. With no
basis, any assignment was as defensible as any other, and an arbitrary one was
cheaper to write than a derived one. The absence of a "why" field is what allowed
a counter to masquerade as engineering.

A second and subtler cause: no way to distinguish a location the mechanism
**fixes** from one it merely **constrains**. Without that distinction, a free
parameter has to be resolved somehow, and resolving it silently asserts a fact
that was never derived.

## Design decision

`LocationBasis` names the mechanical fact that places a feature: shared boundary,
common axis, coaxial overlap, motion corridor, motion extreme, reaction site,
engaged-state contact, access crossing. `RELATION_BASIS` maps each relation kind to
its basis and sits in the knowledge base — that a revolute pair lies on the axis
its members share is true of any product containing one.

`LocationDerivation` carries `determined`, `free_parameters` and `alternatives`.
A guide is **determined**: it spans the whole corridor its body travels, and no
other span is valid. A bearing station is **constrained but free**: any station on
the shared axis transfers the same reaction.

`SEPARATED_ALONG_AXIS` replaces the counter with its actual mechanical content:
two reactions of one kind on one element must occupy distinct stations. It is
emitted only when such a pair exists — which is why BM-002 emits none, correctly.

Two realization checks were rewritten to test the demand rather than the
assignment. `AXIAL_REACTION_STATION` now asks whether the reactor is on the shared
axis, not whether it sits at a nominated end; `CONTACT_AT_EXTREME` asks whether the
body has a bounded travel at all.

## Stage ownership

| Field | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `RELATION_BASIS` | knowledge base | that a limit acts at a travel extreme is mechanical |
| `LocationDerivation` | schema | shared between the stage that derives and the stages that consume |
| derivation *resolution* | Stage 04 | which axis, which face, which host is a placement question |
| geometry | renderer | follows the anchor it is given, decides nothing |

Nothing was fixed in the renderer. The renderer was never the problem; it was
faithfully drawing an assignment that had already been made.

## Generalization

The basis is looked up by relation kind and permitted motion, never by element
name. A test asserts every `SpatialRelationKind` has both an anchor rule and a
basis rule, so a new relation cannot be added without stating where it lives and
why.

Seven tests answer the three verification questions on a synthetic linear carrier
that appears in no benchmark: every anchor cites a basis and explains it; every
anchor names the relationship and participants that determined it; an undetermined
location declares its free parameters and alternatives; a determined location
declares none. One test reverses the declaration order of every element and asserts
the placement stamp is unchanged - the direct regression test for the defect.

## Validation performed

| | BM-001 | BM-002 |
|---|---|---|
| anchors citing a basis | **13/13** | **19/19** |
| determined | 10 | 12 |
| constrained but free | 3 | 7 |
| bases used | 6 distinct | 7 distinct |

**227 deterministic tests pass.** Source-text invariance, absence of benchmark
lookup and deterministic rendering all retained.

## Freeze status

Not frozen. Stage 05 still does not read anchors or derivations.

## Remaining issues

- Face assignment still comes from a default table (`DEFAULT_FACE`) rather than
  from mechanics. Which face carries the operating role is a product decision, but
  it is currently a convention presented as a result.
- `ZONE_BY_REGION_KIND` and `ZONE_BY_OBLIGATION` still place *bodies* by table.
  Bodies are the remaining category placed by assignment rather than derivation.
- A joint with three or more hosts still uses only the first two.
- `determined=True` is asserted per rule, not proven; nothing checks that a
  location claimed as determined actually admits only one placement.

## Next validation target

Body placement. Every localized feature now derives its location from a
relationship, but the bodies those relationships hold between are still placed by
a role-to-zone table - the same defect one level up.
